# Israel and Palestine

Part of [[World Affairs]]. Written 15-11-2026 from the sources linked below; anything recent is dated.

## In one paragraph
Two peoples, Israeli Jews and Palestinian Arabs, both see the same small strip of land between the Jordan river and the Mediterranean as their homeland. Israel was created there in 1948 and has controlled all of it since 1967. Palestinians live under Israeli occupation in the West Bank, under siege and now war in Gaza, or as citizens inside Israel and refugees abroad. They want a state of their own; Israel says it can't allow one that threatens its security. The current war began when Hamas attacked Israel on 07-10-2023. A ceasefire has officially held since 10-10-2025, but fighting, deaths and the occupation of most of Gaza continue, and the next steps (Hamas giving up its weapons, Israel pulling out, rebuilding) are stuck.

## Key terms
- **Israel:** a Jewish-majority state of about 10 million people, created in 1948. About a fifth of its citizens are Arab.
- **Palestinians:** the Arab people of the area, around 5 million of them in the West Bank and Gaza, plus millions of refugees in nearby countries.
- **West Bank:** land west of the Jordan river, taken by Israel from Jordan in 1967. About 3 million Palestinians and around 500,000 Israeli settlers live there (plus more in East Jerusalem).
- **Gaza (the Gaza Strip):** a tiny coastal strip of about 2 million Palestinians, taken from Egypt in 1967. Israel pulled its settlers out in 2005 but kept control of its borders, air and sea.
- **Settlements:** Israeli towns built on occupied land. Most countries and the UN say they break international law; Israel disputes this.
- **Occupation:** military control of land taken in war. The UN's top court said in 2024 that Israel's occupation is unlawful.
- **Two-state solution:** the long-standing idea of an independent Palestine next to Israel. Most of the world backs it; the current Israeli government and Hamas both reject it, for opposite reasons.
- **Hamas:** an Islamist armed group that has ruled Gaza since 2007. It rejects Israel's right to exist. The UK, US and EU class it as a terrorist organisation.
- **Palestinian Authority (PA):** the more moderate Palestinian government that runs parts of the West Bank, led by Mahmoud Abbas (aged 91). Hamas and the PA are rivals.
- **IDF:** the Israel Defense Forces, Israel's military.

## Key people
- **Benjamin Netanyahu:** Israel's prime minister and longest-serving leader, from the right-wing Likud party. Also on trial at home for corruption, and wanted by the International Criminal Court over the war (he denies wrongdoing).
- **Gadi Eisenkot:** a former army chief now leading Israel's opposition. Asked to try to form the next government (see below).
- **Mahmoud Abbas:** president of the Palestinian Authority.
- **Donald Trump:** US president. The US is Israel's most important ally and arms supplier, and Trump's plan is the basis of the current ceasefire.
- **Ali Shaath:** head of the Palestinian technocrat committee meant to run Gaza day to day (the NCAG, below).

## How we got here
- **Late 1800s to 1947:** Jewish immigration grows under the Zionist movement, which seeks a Jewish homeland. Britain rules the land (the "British Mandate") from 1920 and makes conflicting promises to both sides. Tensions and violence rise; the Holocaust adds urgency to the Jewish case.
- **1947-49:** The UN proposes splitting the land into two states. Jewish leaders accept and declare Israel in 1948; Arab leaders reject it and neighbouring states attack. Israel wins. About 700,000 Palestinians flee or are driven out, which Palestinians call the **Nakba** ("catastrophe"). Their descendants are today's refugees.
- **1967 (Six-Day War):** Israel takes the West Bank, East Jerusalem, Gaza and more. The occupation and settlement-building start here.
- **1987 and 2000:** two Palestinian uprisings (**intifadas**), the second marked by suicide bombings and heavy Israeli military response.
- **1993-95 (Oslo Accords):** Israel and the Palestinians recognise each other and create the PA, meant as a step to a state. Final talks fail by 2000.
- **2005-07:** Israel leaves Gaza; Hamas wins elections in 2006 and seizes Gaza from the PA in 2007. Israel and Egypt impose a blockade. Several short wars follow (2008, 2012, 2014, 2021).
- **07-10-2023:** Hamas-led fighters attack southern Israel, killing about 1,200 people, mostly civilians, and taking 251 hostages. It is the deadliest day in Israel's history.
- **2023-25 (Gaza war):** Israel's campaign to destroy Hamas devastates Gaza. Over 74,000 Palestinians have been killed according to Gaza's health ministry (whose figures don't separate fighters from civilians but have broadly matched independent checks), most of the population is displaced, and UN-backed experts declared famine in parts of Gaza in 2025. South Africa has brought a genocide case at the UN's top court; Israel rejects the charge and says it is fighting a war of self-defence against a group that hides among civilians. A short ceasefire in early 2025 collapsed.
- **September 2025:** the UK, France, Canada, Australia and others formally recognise a State of Palestine.
- **10-10-2025:** a ceasefire under Trump's 20-point plan starts. All living hostages come home that month in exchange for Palestinian prisoners; remains of dead hostages follow over later months.
- **November 2025:** the UN Security Council (Resolution 2803) backs the plan's **Board of Peace**, a Trump-chaired body overseeing Gaza until at least December 2027.

## Where things stand (mid-November 2026)
- **The ceasefire is fragile.** Israeli strikes have continued almost daily; more than 1,400 Palestinians were killed between the ceasefire and October 2026 ([Al Jazeera, 10-10-2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/10/what-has-trumps-board-of-peace-achieved-in-a-year-since-gaza)). Israel says it is hitting Hamas fighters who threaten its troops or break the truce.
- **Israel holds about 60% of Gaza**, more than the roughly 50% the plan allowed, behind a boundary called the "Yellow Line" (Al Jazeera, as above).
- **Phase two is stuck on sequencing.** Israel says Hamas must disarm before reconstruction and further withdrawal; Netanyahu says Hamas "has not surrendered a single weapon". Hamas says it will hand weapons to the Palestinian committee but won't fully disarm while Israel keeps attacking and occupying ([Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/435985); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/10/what-has-trumps-board-of-peace-achieved-in-a-year-since-gaza); [Eurasia Review, 13-11-2026](https://eurasiareview.com/13112026-stalemate-in-gaza-oped-2)). The Board of Peace published a 15-point disarmament roadmap in July 2026 ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/international/6002266-hamas-disarmament-roadmap-unveiled/)).
- **The international force hasn't arrived.** A US-led stabilisation force (planned 20,000 troops and 12,000 police, with Indonesia, Morocco and others pledging people) has not deployed. The Palestinian committee (NCAG) still works from Cairo and hasn't been let into Gaza (Al Jazeera, as above).
- **Aid and rebuilding lag.** Around 227 aid lorries a day enter against a target of 600, about 1.4 million people face hunger, and reconstruction (estimated at about $70bn) has barely started (Al Jazeera, as above).
- **West Bank:** settlement building is speeding up, including plans for the sensitive **E1** area near Jerusalem, which critics say would split the West Bank in two; the UN Security Council is due to discuss it. Settler attacks on Palestinians continue, while the army has also cleared some illegal outposts ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/middle-east/israel-advances-plans-for-895-new-settlement-homes-across-west-bank-palestinian-commission-3011867); [Times of Israel live blog, 12-11-2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-november-12-2026/); [Al Jazeera West Bank coverage](https://www.aljazeera.com/tag/occupied-west-bank/)).
- **Israel's election (27-10-2026) was a draw.** Netanyahu's bloc won 53 of 120 seats and Eisenkot's 48, with Arab-led parties holding the balance; 61 are needed to govern. Likud is still the biggest party (27). President Isaac Herzog asked Eisenkot to form a government after 67 MPs backed him; he has 28 days, extendable by 14. Netanyahu is now calling for a unity government ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/israel/israels-final-election-results-show-neither-netanyahu-rival-can-immedi-rcna606290); [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/hjnatbkaml); [Al Jazeera, 14-11-2026](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/14/netanyahu-says-only-a-unity-government-can-guarantee-israels-security)).
- **The wider region is at war.** The US and Israel have been fighting Iran (Hamas's and Hezbollah's main backer) since 28-02-2026, with a ceasefire and a June deal that both broke down; Israel is also fighting Hezbollah in Lebanon ([House of Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10521/); [CFR tracker](https://www.cfr.org/global-conflict-tracker/conflict/confrontation-between-united-states-and-iran)). This deserves its own explainer.

## The arguments, fairly
- **Israel's case:** it was attacked on 7 October and must make sure it can never happen again; Hamas hides among civilians, so civilian deaths are Hamas's responsibility; it can't withdraw or allow a Palestinian state while armed groups remain; Jews have deep historic ties to the land.
- **Palestinians' case:** they have lived under occupation for almost 60 years without basic rights; settlements are swallowing land meant for their state; the scale of death and destruction in Gaza is collective punishment; they are also indigenous to the land and many were expelled in 1948.
- **Widely shared outside view:** most governments condemn both the 7 October attack and the conduct of the war, oppose settlements, and back a two-state solution, but disagree on how hard to press Israel.
- **Contested facts:** casualty figures, whether specific strikes were lawful, and the genocide question are all disputed. Treat any single number or claim with care and check who is making it.

## What to watch
1. Whether Eisenkot can form a government by mid-December, and if a new government changes Gaza or settlement policy.
2. Whether Hamas disarmament and the stabilisation force actually start (phase two).
3. The UN Security Council meeting on E1, and E1 building bids due 01-12-2026.
4. Aid levels and hunger in Gaza over the winter.
5. How the Iran and Lebanon fighting spills into Gaza and the West Bank.
