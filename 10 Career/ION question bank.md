## Part 3: The responsibilities

### Monitoring and operating trading platforms that run 24/7

1. How would you stay focused monitoring a system for hours?
2. What would you do if something went wrong at 3am and nobody was around?
3. Have you ever worked unusual hours?

**Pointers:** routine of regular checks, not just waiting for alarms. Follow the procedure, escalate to whoever is on call, write down what happened and when. Bershka shifts already vary, with weekends and late finishes.

### Spotting inconsistencies, errors and unusual behaviour

1. Tell me about a time you spotted an error others missed.
2. How do you check your own work?
3. What sort of errors do you think happen on a trading platform?
4. Here's a list of prices. What would make you suspicious?

**Pointers:** dissertation data problem: spotted it when checking before the neural network, rebuilt from several sources after FBref lost its Opta data. Backups: half-time pitch-zone fix; rebuilding Money Buys Points after finding errors. Checking: totals against a second source, sense-check extremes. Platform errors: wrong odds (a favourite with long odds), duplicate trades, a market still open after the result, a wrong currency or time zone, a stake far bigger than normal, probabilities adding up to under 100%.

### Developing insights into customer, counterparty and market behaviour

1. How would you spot that a counterparty is behaving differently from normal?
2. Tell me about a time you found a trend in data.
3. What's the difference between a real pattern and noise?

**Pointers:** compare against their usual volumes, stake sizes, times and markets, then flag the outliers. Share price project: five rules for patterns in time series. Business simulation: three significant trends from 150+ responses. Noise: check the sample size and whether the pattern holds over time; statistical testing.

### Liaising with traders, counterparties and trading venues

1. How would you tell a trader you think they've made a mistake?
2. How do you explain something technical to someone non-technical?
3. A counterparty's figures don't match ours. What do you do?

**Pointers:** be quick, polite and specific: show the evidence, not blame. Jigsaw24 project: presented findings to the partner. Counterparty: check our side first (timing, currency, duplicates), then contact them with the exact differences.

### Helping troubleshoot issues in the trade cycle

1. Talk me through how you'd troubleshoot a problem you've never seen before.
2. Tell me about a time something went wrong and you had to fix it.
3. Two problems at once: what do you do?

**Pointers:** steps: what changed, when did it start, can I reproduce it, check against a second source, fix or escalate, write it down. Example: the dissertation dataset. Priority: most money at risk first, tell the team which one you're on.

### Collaborating with developers and traders to improve the platform

1. Have you ever suggested a better way of doing something?
2. If you kept seeing the same error, what would you do?

**Pointers:** internship: recommended process improvements. Repeated errors: log them with examples and tell the developers, so it gets fixed at the source rather than by hand every time.

## Part 4: What you bring

### BSc in a mathematical or scientific subject

1. How has your degree prepared you for this role?
2. What did your degree teach you about probability?

**Pointers:** high 2:1 Maths and Sports Science. They like hard degrees (maths, economics, statistics). Probability and Statistical Inference module; dissertation turned odds into probabilities.

### Microsoft Office, particularly Excel

1. How would you rate your Excel, out of 10?
2. Which functions do you use most?
3. What's the difference between VLOOKUP and INDEX/MATCH?
4. How would you find duplicates?
5. How would you rearrange a dataset using only formulas? *(face-to-face task style)*
6. Have you used pivot tables? What for?

**Pointers:** give an honest number (about 7 if you've done tonight's practice) and back it up. Pivot tables, VLOOKUP, SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, IF, IFERROR. VLOOKUP only looks right; INDEX/MATCH can look left and doesn't break if columns move. Duplicates: COUNTIF over 1, or conditional formatting. Arranging: LARGE or SMALL to get values in order, INDEX/MATCH to bring the rest of the row; in Excel 365, SORT and FILTER. Practice: the Excel functions workbook.

### Coming to grips with new applications quickly

1. Tell me about a time you learned new software fast.
2. How do you go about learning a new tool?

**Pointers:** taught yourself Tableau, published two dashboards. Learned Dartfish, Minitab, Statistica, MATLAB at uni. Method: a real task first, documentation and examples, ask someone who knows it.

### Attention to detail ("missing a single character can make all the difference")

1. Give me an example of your attention to detail.
2. Spot the difference: GBP 1,250.50 and GBP 1,250.05.
3. How do you avoid mistakes when you're tired or busy?

**Pointers:** the dataset story. The pence are swapped. Checklists, re-checking before sending, a second source, taking a moment rather than rushing.

### A high degree of numeracy, including probabilities

1. Quick-fire: 30% of 200, 145 to the power 0, square root of 121, the brother's age puzzle.
2. Convert odds of 2.50 into a probability.
3. Two independent events at 60% and 50%: chance both happen?
4. A £100,000 house falls 30%. What rise gets it back?

**Pointers:** 60, 1, 11, 22. 40%. 30%. 42.9%. More in section 7 of the cheat sheet. Say your working out loud.

3. Convert €200 to pounds at 1.15.

### Comfortable making informed trading decisions

1. Tell me about a decision you made using data or odds.
2. What's a value bet?
3. When would you not act on your own and escalate instead?

**Pointers:** dissertation: if the model's probability beats the bookmaker's implied probability, that's value; tested over two live matchweeks. Reselling decisions only if you're happy to use them. Escalate when it's outside your authority or the money at risk is big; when unsure, ask.

### Good organisational skills and keeping colleagues updated

1. How do you organise your work?
2. How would you hand over to the next shift?
3. Tell me about a time you kept a team informed.

**Pointers:** internship: weekly routine and the month-end deadline. Handover: what's open, what's been done, anything to watch, written down. Football captain: organising players and passing on information.

### SQL

2. What does a SELECT statement do?
3. How would you learn SQL on the job?

**Pointers:** honest: basics, doing a course now. SELECT pulls columns from a table; WHERE filters rows; GROUP BY totals by group. Learn through shadowing and real queries. ION train SQL on the job.

### Strong interpersonal skills and a mature, responsible attitude

2. When have you been trusted with responsibility?
3. Tell me about a mistake you made.

**Pointers:** a real Bershka story. Youth cricket coaching; confidential data on the Jigsaw24 project. Mistake: small, real, what you did, what you check now.
