## 2. The trainee role

**From their careers site *(site)***

- They hire trainees and promote from within: "every new starter begins on a level playing field". Progression depends on "attitude, work ethic and results", not experience.
- **Training:** 100+ hours in your first few months, given by the leadership team and founders. It covers recruitment basics, sales, business development, **networking technology** and client management. Then the **HB Hub** online platform: 70+ videos and 100+ hours of content.
- **Performance coach, Rhonda D'Ambrosio** (with them since 2019, when they were 12 people). You get a 1-2-1 with her before group training, then a rookie cohort with workshops. After that she does desk coaching: listening to your calls and giving feedback.
- **First two years, "like a graduate scheme":**
  - 0 to 6 months: learn the fundamentals.
  - 6 to 12 months: become a **360 recruiter**, working with both candidates and clients.
  - 12 to 24 months: build your own market.
- **What you learn, three things at once:**
  - **Technology:** the market you recruit for.
  - **Recruitment:** contract vs permanent, plus jargon like IR35 and RTW.
  - **Sales:** open and closed questions, listening, using silence.
- **Promotion targets** (London, total amount billed):

| Level | Billing target | Average time to reach it |
|---|---|---|
| Consultant | £40,000 | 6 months |
| Senior Consultant | £110,000 (£150,000 in total) | 1 year 5 months |
| Principal Consultant | £150,000 (£300,000 in total) | 2 years 2 months |
| Team Leader / Manager | £200,000 (£500,000 in total) | 3 years |

- **Incentives:** uncapped commission, employee share scheme, daily KPI spins, weekly competitions, a split-deal structure, and a Rolex for £1m billers.
- **Monthly clubs:** for example, **40 hours of phone time** in a month earns a team breakfast. The other clubs are lunches and trips (Dubrovnik, Dubai, Ibiza, Phuket, Mexico). That tells you **phone time is measured**: expect daily call targets (KPIs).
- **Office:** gym, showers, PS5, beer fridge, podcast studio, live sales screens, dog-friendly. Football, running and padel clubs. Extended Christmas break.
- **Founders' welcome note:** they admit "the first year can be challenging". They also offer help with accommodation, credit, loans and mortgages.

**Pay (confirmed by Oliver, 23-11-2026, [[2026-11-23 Hamilton Barnes - Salary and commission]]):** **£26,000 basic**, year 1 OTE **£32,000 to £35,000**, commission uncapped from day 1 at 5% of the fee per side (10% if you do both). Replaces the older listing figures of £35,000 to £45,000 OTE.

**What the job is, in plain terms:** you find candidates (engineers) for clients (companies hiring), mostly by phone and LinkedIn. You earn a fee when someone is placed, and you get commission on that. A **360** consultant also wins the clients; a **180** focuses on one side. Expect cold calling, targets and lots of rejection, but your pay grows with what you bill.

## 3. Likely questions and pointers

**Tell me about yourself**
Maths and Sports Science graduate, Nottingham Trent, 2026. Customer-facing at Bershka. Reselling on the side (buying, pricing, selling). Football captain, cricket coach. Looking for a career where effort turns into results.

**Why recruitment?**

- You like working with people, and being judged on results.
- Uncapped commission: earnings track effort.
- It's fast-paced and competitive, which suits you (sport, captaincy).
- Reselling taught you pricing, negotiating and dealing with buyers.

**Why Hamilton Barnes?**

- A specialist, so you'd become an expert in one market rather than a generalist.
- Proper training and promotion from within: everyone started as a trainee.
- FT fastest-growing and Sunday Times Best Place to Work: growth plus culture.
- Tech markets suit your maths background. You can understand technical candidates.
- Specific things from their site you can mention: Rhonda's 1-2-1 coaching and call feedback; the open promotion targets ("I like that progression isn't a guessing game"); 90+ people grown from two founders in a cupboard; offices in Austin and KL, so there's room to grow; AI and data centres are booming markets.

**Why tech recruitment with a maths degree?**
You're comfortable with technical subjects and numbers, and recruitment is a business: targets, conversion rates, pipelines. You'd enjoy learning a technical market.

**Are you money-motivated?**
Honestly yes, and that's what they want to hear. Pair it with wanting to build a career, not just a pay cheque.

**Resilience / handling rejection**
The job hunt: final rounds that didn't convert, rejections, and you kept going and improved each time. Recruitment is full of "no".

**Competitive / hitting targets**
Football captain. Reselling profit. Bershka sales or busy periods (a real example).

**Persuading someone / sales example**
Reselling: pricing an item, handling offers, closing a sale. Or Bershka: helping a customer decide.

**Working under pressure, long hours**
Bershka rota with weekends and late finishes.

**"Sell me this pen" or a role-play**
Ask questions first ("What do you use a pen for? What annoys you about your current one?"), then match the benefits to their answers, then ask for the sale. Asking before pitching is the skill.

**What do you know about our markets?**
Network engineers keep company networks running; cybersecurity protects them; cloud and data centres host the systems; telecoms covers mobile and fibre networks. Keep it simple; they don't expect expertise.

**What else are you applying for?**
"Mostly data and trading operations roles; I'm in process with a sports trading firm." Honest and normal.

**Salary and start date**
In line with the trainee package. Start after your Bershka notice period (check it).

**Where do you see yourself in 2 to 3 years?**
Use their ladder: Consultant within about 6 months, Senior Consultant by about 18 months, then Principal, maybe helping train new starters.

**Also expect**
- **What motivates you?** Money, honestly, plus competition, progression and building your own market.
- **Tell me about a goal you set yourself and hit.**
- **How do you handle a bad day?** Reset, learn from it, go again (their "resilience" word).
- **How would you feel about cold calling all day?** Comfortable. Their 40-hour phone time club is about 2 hours of conversation a day (40 hours over 20 to 22 working days), which could mean 60 to 100+ dials.
- **What does a recruiter actually do?** Find candidates, win clients, match them, get paid on placement; 360 means doing both sides.
- **A short role-play call**, for example ringing a network engineer about a job: introduce yourself, ask about their current role and what would make them move, listen, then suggest a next step.
- **Can you get to Liverpool Street every day?**

**Practise out loud tonight:** "Why recruitment?" and "Tell me about a time you faced rejection."

**Money check:** the promotion targets are what you **bill** (fees to the company), not your pay. You earn basic plus commission on billings. Ask Oliver how commission works for trainees.
