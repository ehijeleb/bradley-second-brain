## 2. The trainee role *(listings)*

- They hire **only at trainee level** and say they never hire anyone above you: everyone up to Director started as a trainee.
- Pay: about **£26,000 basic + uncapped commission**, first-year OTE about **£35,000 to £45,000**.
- Training: **100+ hours** of in-house training in the first six months, including from the CEO and co-founder; an online platform with 70+ training videos; some ads mention a performance coach.
- Progression targets (from The Scoop): about **£40k** of billings to go from trainee to consultant, **£110k** to senior consultant, **£150k** to principal consultant. Their careers site claims 78% of Associate Consultants are promoted within 5 months.
- Perks mentioned: regular incentives, socials, monthly competitions, office gym access in some ads.
- Requirements in ads: within about an hour's commute of Liverpool Street, or willing to relocate.

**What the job is, in plain terms:** you find candidates (engineers) for clients (companies hiring), mostly by phone and LinkedIn. You earn a fee when someone is placed, and you get commission on that. A **360** consultant also wins the clients; a **180** focuses on one side. Expect cold calling, targets and lots of rejection, but your pay grows with what you bill.

## 3. Likely questions and pointers

**Tell me about yourself**
Maths and Sports Science graduate, Nottingham Trent, 2026. Customer-facing at Bershka. Reselling on the side (buying, pricing, selling). Football captain, cricket coach. Looking for a career where effort turns into results.

**Why recruitment?**

- You like working with people, and being judged on results.
- Uncapped commission: earnings track effort.
- It's fast-paced and competitive, which suits you (sport, captaincy).
- Reselling taught you pricing, negotiating and dealing with buyers.

**Why Hamilton Barnes?**

- A specialist, so you'd become an expert in one market rather than a generalist.
- Proper training and promotion from within: everyone started as a trainee.
- FT fastest-growing and Sunday Times Best Place to Work: growth plus culture.
- Tech markets suit your maths background. You can understand technical candidates.

**Why tech recruitment with a maths degree?**
You're comfortable with technical subjects and numbers, and recruitment is a business: targets, conversion rates, pipelines. You'd enjoy learning a technical market.

**Are you money-motivated?**
Honestly yes, and that's what they want to hear. Pair it with wanting to build a career, not just a pay cheque.

**Resilience / handling rejection**
The job hunt: final rounds that didn't convert, rejections, and you kept going and improved each time. Recruitment is full of "no".

**Competitive / hitting targets**
Football captain. Reselling profit. Bershka sales or busy periods (a real example).

**Persuading someone / sales example**
Reselling: pricing an item, handling offers, closing a sale. Or Bershka: helping a customer decide.

**Working under pressure, long hours**
Bershka rota with weekends and late finishes.

**"Sell me this pen" or a role-play**
Ask questions first ("What do you use a pen for? What annoys you about your current one?"), then match the benefits to their answers, then ask for the sale. Asking before pitching is the skill.

**What do you know about our markets?**
Network engineers keep company networks running; cybersecurity protects them; cloud and data centres host the systems; telecoms covers mobile and fibre networks. Keep it simple; they don't expect expertise.

**What else are you applying for?**
"Mostly data and trading operations roles; I'm in process with a sports trading firm." Honest and normal.

**Salary and start date**
In line with the trainee package. Start after your Bershka notice period (check it).

**Where do you see yourself in 2 to 3 years?**
Promoted to consultant or senior consultant, billing consistently, maybe helping train new starters.
