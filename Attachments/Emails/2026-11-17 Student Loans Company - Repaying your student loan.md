Hi Bradley,
Depending on how much you earn, you may be due to start making repayments to your student loan from April 2027. We want to let you know what to expect and remind you about the repayment process.

**Get to know your new student loan repayment account!**
You can use your online account to: check your balance, update your personal information, make extra repayments, view your statements, chat to us.

You should use the same details as the account you used to apply for student finance.

You must keep your name, address and contact details up to date so that we can contact you with important information about your loan or repayments.

If the email address linked to your account is a university or college one, you should update it to a personal email instead.

**How much will I repay?**
Student loans aren't like other types of borrowing. If you don't earn above the repayment threshold, you won't make any repayments towards your loan.

You'll repay 9% of your income above the repayment threshold. From 6 April 2027 the repayment threshold will be £26,025 a year, £2,168 a month or £500 a week.

| Income each year before tax | Student loan monthly repayment |
| --- | --- |
| £26,025 | £0 |
| £27,500 | £11 |
| £30,000 | £29 |
| £32,500 | £48 |
| £35,000 | £67 |
| £40,000 | £104 |

Your repayments will change as your income changes. For example, if you earn more one month because you've been paid a bonus or worked overtime, then your repayments for that month will increase too.

**How will I make repayments?**
If you're employed, your employer will take repayments directly from your salary at the same time as Income Tax and National Insurance. Always check your payslip to make sure your student loan repayments are correct.

If you're not making repayments but you think you should be, speak to your employer.

If you're self-employed, you'll make repayments at the same time as you pay Income Tax and National Insurance.

Any loan balance remaining 40 years after you're due to start repaying will be written off.

**What interest am I charged?**
Interest has been charged since you were paid your first instalment and will be applied monthly until your loan has been paid in full or cancelled. The interest rate is based on the UK Retail Price Index (RPI) and varies depending on your circumstances.

**What if I'm planning to go travelling or move abroad?**
If you're planning to leave the UK for more than three months you need to let us know before you go. You'll need to make repayments to us directly rather than through HMRC if you're earning over the threshold. It's important that you keep us updated, even if you're not working while you're out the country.

**Already made a student loan repayment and wondering why?**
This isn't very common but can happen if your employer has used the wrong information about when you should start to repay. If they've taken any student loan repayments before April 2027, you can ask us for a refund. When you contact us, you'll need to have your employer's name and your Customer Reference Number (CRN).

**Thinking of making extra repayments?**
You can make extra repayments to your loan if you want to. Before you do this, it's important to think about your personal and financial circumstances and how these might change. Any extra repayments you make can't be refunded.

If you don't fully expect to repay your outstanding balance during the term of the loan you should carefully consider whether it's appropriate to make voluntary repayments because any outstanding balance is written off at the end of the loan term. If you're unsure about your decision to make voluntary repayments, you should seek professional advice from a financial advisor.
